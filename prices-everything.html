Everything the rolls price

97,959 entries in the Patent, Close, Fine, Charter and Liberate Rolls name money; 59,312 are priced: a sum the act itself pays, owes or charges, each act counted once. 3,421 more name only an editor's page reference, a membrane's number or a weight that the scan read as money.

What "priced" means

Every sum in an entry is given a role from the words just before and after it. A sum is priced when its role is a price paid to the king, a fine for an offence, a farm or yearly render, a debt to the king, a payment by the king, a loan, or a debt between subjects. The other roles are money the act only names: the value of land or goods, a rent, a tax rate, a sum sued for, a penalty, money moved between the king's offices. They are kept, as data of their own, but are not prices. 15,608 entries have a sum whose role the rules cannot read yet.

How this was made
  1. Searched. Every canonical entry of the five rolls (a second reading of one act counts once, as the corpus counts it), read by rules: nothing here was written by a model.
  2. The kind. From the act's opening words, the first rule to speak among the kinds of what the king took, debts between subjects, then what he paid; an act no rule names is "not yet sorted". Each kind's page gives its rule as written.
  3. The sum. What the king took: the first sum a price word leads into, not a value, a fee, a rent or a penalty. What he paid: the first sum that is no value or rent. Then that sum's own words give its role (the table above); a sum whose role is no price is set aside and the entry's other sums are asked in turn. Below 3s. 4d. a price is a rent, a fee or a misreading. The scan's "4 mark" is half a mark.
  4. The unit. Once, a day or a year, from the words after the sum.
  5. Counted. Each act once. 932 priced entries are a second scan of one printed book (the same sum, the words around it agreeing 8 letters in 10, at the same place in the book) and 111 the act on another roll (one kind and sum, not an "other" kind, within three years, naming a person the corpus names in 50 entries or fewer); each is set aside for the entry it copies. Read entry by entry on 18 November 2026: 20 second scans drawn at random were each one act read again; of all 110 pairs on two rolls, 99 were one grant met again (an order to pay its arrears, a confirmation), 10 two acts (4 an office passing to its next holder at the same wage) and 1 unclear.
  6. Checked. The roles' rules were written from 80 entries read whole, then each role was checked by reading 8 to 10 of its entries (the right-hand column); every fault found is now a rule with a test that fails without it. The kinds have not been read entry by entry, except the two with pages of their own (wards, confirmations).
