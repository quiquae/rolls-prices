How this was made
  1. Searched. Every canonical entry of the Patent, Close, Fine, Charter and Liberate Rolls (a second reading of the same act is counted once, as the corpus does). Here: the entries whose opening words this kind's rule matches, the first rule to speak among the kinds tried in order (what the king took, then debts between subjects, then what he paid). 1,598 of them name money.
  2. The sum. For this kind: the first sum a price word leads into (for, by fine of, paying, render), not the value of land, a fee, a rent or a penalty, and at least 3s. 4d.. Then the words just before and after that sum are asked what it is (its role): a sum whose role is no price (the value or rent of land, a tax, a sum sued for, a penalty, money moved, a page reference) is set aside, and the entry's other sums are asked in turn. 1,303 are priced.
  3. The unit. Once, a day or a year, from the words after the sum ("a day", "yearly", "for life ... at the Exchequer"). This page shows the 1,073 sums paid a year; the others are in the table of roles.
  4. Counted. Each entry once. A grant enrolled on two rolls, or recited later, is here twice; the ward and confirmation series count such copies once, and this kind is not yet so counted.
  5. Real terms. "At 1300 prices" divides by Gregory Clark's price index; "years of pay" by a worker's yearly earnings in his series (MeasuringWorth).
  6. Read by hand. The roles were checked by reading 8 to 10 entries of each, the latest reading after the last change: a price paid to the king 9 of 10, a payment by the king 7 of 8, a farm or render 7 of 8, a debt to the king 7 of 8, a value or rent 7 of 8; a debt between subjects, a loan, a fine for an offence, a sum sued for, a tax, a penalty, money moved and not money 8 of 8 each. This kind has not been read entry by entry.
