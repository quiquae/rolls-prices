What was sold

A marriage alone cost a median 60 marks; the wardship of the lands with the marriage 80; the wardship alone 100 (48 sales). Between 1300-49 and 1350-99 the median marriage fell from 60 to 30 marks, and wardship with marriage from 70 to 40. Both rose again after 1400 (100 and 82).

The money

  • A mark is a sum, not a coin: 13s. 4d., two-thirds of a pound, 160 pence, for the whole period. Every sum is turned into pence and shown in marks.
  • Prices hardly moved. On Gregory Clark's index a basket of goods cost about the same in 1450 as in 1300, so "at 1300 prices" changes little.
  • Pay doubled after the Black Death. The same price was about half as much work, so "years of a worker's pay" shows the fall after 1350 deeper: 26, 7, then 16 years.
  • The penny lost silver: over 22 grains until 1344, 18 by 1351, 15 by 1412, 12 by 1464 (Coins of the pound sterling). Clark's index is in pence as paid, so this is already in it.
