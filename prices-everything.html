Prices in the Patent Rolls

Everything the rolls price

95,625 entries in the Patent, Close, Fine, Charter and Liberate Rolls name money; 59,164 are priced: a sum the act itself pays, owes or charges. 3,489 more name only an editor's page reference, a membrane's number or a weight that the scan read as money.

What "priced" means

Every sum in an entry is given a role from the words just before and after it. A sum is priced when its role is a price paid to the king, a fine for an offence, a farm or yearly render, a debt to the king, a payment by the king, a loan, or a debt between subjects. The other roles are money the act only names: the value of land or goods, a rent, a tax rate, a sum sued for, a penalty, money moved between the king's offices. They are kept, as data of their own, but are not prices. 15,111 entries have a sum whose role the rules cannot read yet.

How this was made
  1. Searched. Every canonical entry of the five rolls (a second reading of one act counts once, as the corpus counts it), read by rules: nothing here was written by a model.
  2. The kind. From the act's opening words, the first rule to speak among the kinds of what the king took, debts between subjects, then what he paid; an act no rule names is "not yet sorted". Each kind's page gives its rule as written.
  3. The sum. What the king took: the first sum a price word leads into, not a value, a fee, a rent or a penalty. What he paid: the first sum that is no value or rent. Then that sum's own words give its role (the table above); a sum whose role is no price is set aside and the entry's other sums are asked in turn. Below 3s. 4d. a price is a rent, a fee or a misreading. The scan's "4 mark" is half a mark.
  4. The unit. Once, a day or a year, from the words after the sum.
  5. Checked. The roles' rules were written from 80 entries read whole, then each role was checked by reading 8 to 10 of its entries (the right-hand column); every fault found is now a rule with a test that fails without it. The kinds have not been read entry by entry, except the two with pages of their own (wards, confirmations), and copies on two rolls are not yet counted once.
